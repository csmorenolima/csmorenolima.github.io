By Royal Decree of Her Majesty

To the Chosen Bearer of Lumyrion

Let it be known throughout the Six Crowns that a grave shadow gathers in the West. Ancient borders fail, once-faithful realms fracture, and corruption now spreads from TarragØn across the Enën Sea.

By the authority of the Crown of Aeyantis, we place into your keeping Lumyrion, Guardian of Order and Balance. You are commanded to journey west, trace the source of the unraveling, and stand against the darkness threatening every crown and shore.

Bear this charge with courage. Cross the wounded realms and confront the power rising within TarragØn before sea, soil and throne are consumed.

Your journey begins where the light ends.

Given beneath the royal seal in the halls of Aeyantis.

PLAYER FOUNDATIONS

User Needs

Scrolls of Odyssey is a PEGI 18 fantasy title designed around a dark, politically fractured world. The experience prioritises deliberate pacing, strong visual hierarchy, and readable interfaces to support long-form exploration, narrative weight and player choice without overwhelming the player.

Engaging Colour

High-contrast palettes and controlled highlights are used to guide attention, signal danger, and reinforce narrative beats. Colour is employed deliberately to distinguish safe spaces, corrupted regions, and moments of consequence.

Mature Visual Tone

Violence is present and narratively significant, reinforcing the game’s darker themes. Encounters are visually clear and impactful, focusing on atmosphere, scale, and consequence rather than gratuitous detail. The aesthetic supports tension, dread, and moral weight.

Clear Direction

Despite the game’s complexity, player actions are communicated clearly. Objectives, interactions, and combat prompts are concise and readable, allowing players to focus on strategy and immersion rather than interface friction.

Readable Text

Typography is built for dark scenes: strong contrast, controlled line-length, clear hierarchy, and consistent spacing. Key prompts remain readable through fog, vignette and heavy atmospherics without breaking immersion.

Player Snapshot

  • Plays in longer sessions (1–3 hours), often at night with headphones.
  • Values worldbuilding, political tension, and choices with consequences.
  • Prefers minimal UI clutter, strong contrast, and clear readability in dark scenes.
  • Explores off the critical path when lore or side regions feel meaningful.

CONCEPTUALISATION

Game Genre

Scrolls of Odyssey combines point-and-click exploration with text-based mechanics to blend visual intuition with deep, narrative-driven interaction. This hybrid style allows for seamless navigation while using text to convey nuanced sensory details and complex choices that visuals alone cannot capture, creating a highly immersive and atmospheric experience.

GAME FOUNDATIONS

UI and Game Design

The interface and game systems are designed to keep the player immersed while making every action easy to understand. Clear navigation, focused interaction areas and restrained visual feedback support exploration, dialogue and decision-making without competing with the world’s atmosphere.

Webpage Properties

The webpage features a clear Title and a simplified Dropdown Menu that provides instant access to the core game loop, including Play Game , Instructions , and About and Credits .
The interface is designed with a centralised Game Window for focused play, supported by a structured Instructions section for quick reference and a Footer for additional developer details.

Game Window

The Game Window serves as the central hub of player activity, beginning with a prompt to Enter Name and Play to establish a session. Once logged in, players transition into the gates of Aeyantis Kingdom, a staging area for navigation and social interaction, which provides direct access to the Blacksmith for acquiring equipment.

GAME OVERVIEW

Game Features

A focused set of systems designed to support atmospheric exploration, narrative clarity, and creature-driven worldbuilding.

Guided Narrative Journey

A story-driven experience with a clear beginning and end, designed to maintain pacing while allowing players to engage with the world at a comfortable, readable pace.

Branching Exploration Paths

While the main journey leads toward TarragØn, optional regions allow players to uncover side stories, creatures, and lore without disrupting the core narrative.

Guardian Relic System (Lumyrion)

A living relic bound to the player that reacts to corruption and choice. Lumyrion offers guidance and protection, but introduces risk and consequence when relied upon too heavily.

Creature-Centric World Design

The Enën Sea is shaped by powerful creatures rather than human rule. Beasts define territories, danger levels, and narrative tone, making the world feel ancient and untamed.

Environmental Storytelling

Lore is embedded directly into landscapes, ruins, and creature domains, encouraging discovery through observation rather than exposition.

Accessible, Readable Interface

A dark, high-contrast UI with strong hierarchy, built to support long-form exploration without overwhelming the player.

I. The Age of Six Crowns

For centuries, the lands surrounding the Enën Sea lived beneath the sovereign of the Six Crowns — Aeyantis, Katzh, Vulcaria, Ame’xore, Dreonathen, and Kithren. Their banners shaped diplomacy, war, and the rhythms of life itself. To the east, prosperity thrived beneath the Crowns’ watchful eyes. To the west, a slow-burning tension grew, layered over generations.

II. The Western Realms

Beyond the reach of eastern courts lay the Western Realms — Dsärr, Edresan, and Yán - lands bound by the laws of the Six, yet never granted the dignity of equal standing. They were expected to uphold treaties they had no voice in shaping, and to honour decisions forged in distant halls where their names were seldom spoken and their counsel never sought. In Dsärr, Zhäro ruled with quiet restraint. He claimed no crown, held no illusions of grandeur. His realm was small, his strength limited, and he believed survival lay in humility. The Six Kingdoms were too vast, their armies too numerous; resistance would only bring ruin. But his son, Zuläch, saw only the weight of humiliation.

III. The War of Sundering

In the age of the Sundering, long before the rise of TarragØn, the world had faced a darkness so absolute that strikes fear in the hearts. From the void beyond memory came Saar, an ancient evil so absolute in its malice that the world could not comprehend it. It swept across land and sea, unmaking everything in it's path. Forests curled inward, seas recoiled from their own shores, and living beings twisted into violent forms no natural law could explain. Kingdoms fell without blade or flame, collapsing into silence as if erased from within. Language faltered as meaning slipped away. Armies marched against Saar and vanished without trace. When the end seemed certain, the scriptures speak of only two vast presences that rose against the unraveling — the Guardians. Not gods, nor angels, nor creatures of flesh, but primordial anchors bound to the world’s foundations, shaping its laws simply by existing. Together, they forged four stabilising truths: ◈ Order - Restrain chaos ◈ Will - Resist corruption ◈ Form - Prevent manifestation ◈ Veil - Conceal and contain These became the Four Eternal Chains, sunk beneath the Mountains of Shal’Mur, binding the wounded world tight against Saar’s wrath. The first three rose in unbroken unity. But as the Guardians began the fourth, Saar struck back — a violent convulsion of destruction that shattered the boundaries of existence. Continents split and Seas blackened. Storms tore open the heavens as the world buckled beneath the clash. In that final moment, Caelthior was drawn into the void, its presence unraveling under Saar’s strike. Yet even as it faded into absence, it held the rising Pillar long enough for the truth to take shape. The Veil — the Fourth and final Pillar rose.

IV. The Old Treaty

In the aftermath of the Sundering, the Six Crowns and the Western Realms forged the Old Treaty — a pact born of fear and necessity. ◈ None would seek Saar ◈ None would attempt to wield Saar ◈ None would disturb the Pillars beneath Shal’Mur ◈ All disputes must be settled without invoking ancient forces The vows were forged together, but over time the Six became their stewards. What they upheld became the measure; what they forbade became the boundary. And the West bore the burden of laws guided by hands not their own. The treaty preserved peace, but froze the political order. The West remained beneath the Six, tied to laws that never granted them respect. Resentment grew like roots beneath stone.

V. Zuläch and the Rise of TarragØn

Zuläch grew up watching his father bow to eastern pressure. Where Zhäro saw wisdom, Zuläch saw weakness. Where Zhäro saw survival, Zuläch saw humiliation. He wanted recognition. He wanted a crown. He wanted the West to stand equal - or above. In secret, he forged alliances with Qadril of Edresan and Domiyán of Yán realm, and together, they orchestrated Zhäro’s death, disguising it as a tragic accident. With his father gone, Zuläch unified the West through threats, promises, and carefully spun lies. The three realms became one, The Kingdom of TarragØn — the First Western-most Kingdom. For the first time, the West held a crown equal to the Six and for a moment, Zuläch’s dream seemed fulfilled.

VI. The Breaking of the Seal

But power sharpened his hunger. Ruling TarragØn was not enough. He wanted permanence - faster armies, larger territories, a kingdom no force could challenge. For that, he sought what the Old Treaty forbade. Beneath Shal’Mur, Zuläch found the ancient seals that imprisoned Saar. The air swelled with whispering voices, beckoning to be freed. Convinced he could control what others had only feared, he broke the ancient protections. He was wrong. Saar did not serve him. Saar consumed him. His body warped into a colossal, nightmarish form. His mind shattered and reformed under Saar’s will. Zuläch became the Mighty Troll of TarragØn - Saar’s hatred wearing his flesh.

VII. Beasts of the Enën Sea

The Enën Sea is a world of many creatures. They answer to instinct, territory, and corruption. Gortusk — Volcanic War-Beast Armoured thundering titans whose charges flatten fortifications, tusks that carve trenches into the earth. They roam volcanic highlands of Vulcaria. Megalore — Colossal Sea Predator A 110‑ft hunter that prowls the coasts, shattering hulls and capsising ships that stray too near TarragØn’s shores. Ly’cara — Mistwood Pack Hunters Shadow-moving predators that stalk the forests of Kithren. Deep Abyssal — Trench-Dwelling Colossi Towering beings rising from the deepest trenches of the Enën Sea, bending currents and darkness. Cyrenthos — Corruption-Bound Entities Ancient beasts dwelling in twisted valleys near Dreonathen's borders.

VIII. The Keeper of Hidden Archives

The first to recognise the pattern was Teri’itaria of Ame’xore, Keeper of the Hidden Archives. She saw echoes of the Sundering in the spreading corruption - Signs recorded only in the oldest vaults, written by those who witnessed Saar’s first rise. She recognised what others refused to see. The return of Saar. Teri’itaria carried this truth to Queen Ahris of Aeyantis, whose resolve could act where others hesitated. Together, they awakened Lumyrion - the last Guardian capable of standing against the corruption rising from TarragØn.

IX. The Call To TarragØn

Within the vaults of Aeyantis lay the rites to awaken the surviving Guardian. Through a forbidden ritual, Queen Ahris summoned Lumyrion and bound its essence into a radiant pendant. Lumyrion does not choose. It answers. When it stirred, its light sought a spirit steady enough to bear its weight — one that did not recoil when all others faltered. Queen Ahris recognised this and placed the pendant in that knight’s hands, a burden the world could no longer carry on its own. With Lumyrion’s light at your side, you begin the long road toward TarragØn. There, beneath a sky warped by ancient malice, waits the Mighty Troll of TarragØn - To face the Troll is to face Saar itself.
